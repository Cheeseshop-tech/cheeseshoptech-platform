# Asiago DOP Consortium Booklet — digitized source

**Owner:** Rick Posada · **Status:** Reference, digitized 2026-08-25 · **Feeds:** `CHEESE_SIGNS_SPEC.md`,
`src/data/montitrentini/signs.json` (the four Asiago records)

**What this is:** Rick photographed all 16 pages of a printed consumer booklet published by the
**Consorzio Tutela Formaggio Asiago** (the Asiago Cheese Regulatory Board — the body the EU
designates to police the Asiago DOP) — *"Asiago Dop Cheese: Get to know it, Recognise it, Love
it."* It is the single most authoritative plain-English source we have on what the DOP actually
requires, because it comes from the body that enforces the designation, not from a retailer or a
producer's marketing page. Source photos: `design/asiago-booklet/reference-photos/` (16 images,
resized/compressed from the originals — good enough to read, not print-resolution; see
"Using the photos" below).

**Why this exists as its own doc:** reading it against `CHEESE_SIGNS_SPEC.md` surfaced one
finding that changes what Open Item #2 in that spec is actually asking — see
[Reconciliation](#reconciliation-against-cheese-signs) below. Everything else here is reference
material for future Asiago copy (signage, web, Media Hub captions) — sensory language straight
from the regulator reads more credibly than anything we'd write from scratch.

## 1. What the booklet says, page by page

*(Photo filenames in `design/asiago-booklet/reference-photos/`, in booklet order.)*

### Cover — `IMG_9788.jpg`
"Asiago Dop Cheese — Get to know it. Recognise it. Love it." Photo of a pyramid of Asiago wheels,
wedges and a cut block on a wood table. Bottom right: the Consorzio Tutela Formaggio Asiago's own
circular emblem (red ring, mountain-peak mark) — the consortium's mark, distinct from the label
printed on individual wheel rinds.

### Region map — `IMG_9789.jpg`
Photo of Holstein cattle on a high alpine pasture (Dolomite peaks in the background), plus a map
of the Veneto/Trentino border area with the DOP production zone shaded orange, spanning Trento,
Vicenza, Belluno and part of Padova provinces around the Asiago plateau, with Bolzano, Verona,
Treviso, Venice and Rovigo marked as the surrounding reference cities.

### "Asiago Dop cheese" — `IMG_9790.jpg` (p.3)
The official **EU PDO seal** (blue/gold, "Denominazione d'Origine Protetta," wheat-sheaf mark and
gold stars) at the top of the page. Body text:

> Asiago has been produced for thousands of years within a geographically well-defined area
> around the Asiago plateau. The cheese Asiago Dop (Protected Designation of Origin) is a
> healthy, wholesome product with a delicious and unmistakeable flavour, guaranteed by the
> Ministry of Agriculture and by the Consorzio Tutela Formaggio Asiago (Asiago Cheese Regulatory
> Board). Asiago Dop is a real friend at your table: suited to maintaining a balanced diet with a
> correct supply of energy and calories.

### "Asiago Fresco Dop Cheese" — `IMG_9791.jpg` (p.4)
> Fresh cheese with a milky flavour (maturing period from 20 to 40 days).

### "Asiago Stagionato Dop Cheese" — `IMG_9792.jpg` (p.5)
> Flavoursome ripened cheese. It is also called:
> **Mezzano** (maturing period from 4 to 6 months)
> **Vecchio** (maturing period over 10 months)
> **Stravecchio** (maturing period over 15 months).

This is the load-bearing fact in the whole booklet — see [Reconciliation](#reconciliation-against-cheese-signs).

### "Asiago Dop — How to recognise it" — `IMG_9793.jpg` (p.6–7)
"The markings and the quality seals of the Asiago cheese are stamped on the side." Callouts on a
whole wheel + cut wedge: the name "ASIAGO" on the side, and the "Dop logo" stamped into the rind.
"All this guarantees the traceability of each cheese."

### Casein stamp closeup — `IMG_9794.jpg` (p.7 continued)
Closeup of the casein stamp embedded in the paste (not just the rind): "D.O.P. Asiago" with a
circular Dop logo and a numeric ID (the photographed example reads *1096908*), captioned "Casein
stamp with the identification number of the whole cheese," plus "Number of the dairy" as a
separate stamp on the rind. This is a second, independent traceability mark from the rind stamp —
worth knowing if a customer ever asks how to verify authenticity beyond the wheel label.

### Asiago Fresco sensory guide — `IMG_9795.jpg`, `IMG_9796.jpg`, `IMG_9797.jpg` (p.9, 11, 13)
- **By sight:** white or very pale straw colour; eye formation is marked and irregular.
- **By smell:** closest reference is a pot of yoghurt and a slab of butter — sniff over both, the
  smells are similar to Fresco Dop.
- **By taste:** neither salty nor bitter, sweet like milk fresh from the cow, slight acidity
  similar to whole yoghurt — "a very simple but highly agreeable taste."
- **By feel:** soft as sponge cake in the hand; does not stick to the fingers and is not even
  greasy; delicate.
- **By chewing:** melts and coats the mouth fast, similar to a meringue — pleasant sweet-acid
  flavour that "creates the desire to have another slice immediately."
- **Recipe — Fantasy salad with Asiago Fresco Dop** (4 servings): 200g Asiago Fresco Dop, 300g
  button mushrooms, 150g streaky bacon, olive oil/salt/pepper, redcurrants or pomegranate seeds to
  garnish. Sauté sliced mushrooms and bacon strips in olive oil until crisp; plate with thin
  Fresco slivers, top with the warm bacon and mushrooms, garnish with pomegranate seeds. 15 min.

### Asiago Stagionato sensory guide — `IMG_9798.jpg`, `IMG_9799.jpg`, `IMG_9800.jpg` (p.15, 17, 19)
- **By sight:** straw to light straw colour; openness/eye formation is small to medium (under 1
  cm).
- **By smell:** closest references are bread or pizza dough, and almonds or hazelnuts.
- **By taste:** decisive, flavoursome. Sweet like a boiled chestnut, with aromatic and salty notes
  that increase toward the rind, and pleasantly spicy hints in more mature examples. "A marked and
  unmistakeable character" common to all Stagionato.
- **By feel:** close-textured yet soft in the youngest examples ("mezzano") — like a frankfurter —
  hardening toward a "Grana"-like texture as it matures further; doesn't stick to the fingers,
  leaves a light, pleasantly greasy finish.
- **By chewing:** should be bitten and calmly chewed, not swallowed quickly — "it is hard like a
  parboiled carrot." Let saliva work on it; the flavor is perfectly soluble and reveals itself
  fully this way.
- **Recipe — Green apple with Asiago Stagionato Dop mousse** (2 servings): 150g Asiago Stagionato
  Dop, 2 green apples, 50g single cream, 2 tsp sugar. Warm the Stagionato with a little cream,
  cool, whip into a mousse. Core and poach the apples in sugar-water until soft, slice into 1cm
  rings, fill/cover with the mousse. 25 min.

### "How to serve it" — `IMG_9801.jpg` (p.21)
Recommended cut: wedge the wheel, then slice each wedge into "plugs" — 7×2×2 cm plugs for Asiago
Fresco Dop, 10×2×2 cm plugs for Asiago Stagionato Dop — fanned on the plate. Serving note: "When
offering Asiago Dop for tasting, it is advisable to present a portion of the cheese with the label
clearly visible, or with the writing stamped onto the rind, so that it is recognisable" — i.e.
always plate with a piece of rind attached bearing the mark.

### "Our recipes" — `IMG_9802.jpg`, `IMG_9803.jpg`
Four more dishes, all Asiago Fresco Dop unless noted:
- **Salad of asparagus, Asiago Fresco Dop and Alto Adige Igp Speck** (2 servings, 20 min)
- **Omelette with Asiago Fresco Dop and seasonal herbs** (2 servings, 20 min)
- **Mushrooms with crusty bread and Asiago Fresco Dop** (2 servings, 20 min)
- **Potato "gnocco" filled with Asiago Fresco Dop fondue** (2 servings, 45 min) — the filling
  actually uses grated **Asiago Stagionato Dop** for the fondue itself.

## 2. Reconciliation against Cheese Signs

`CHEESE_SIGNS_SPEC.md` §4 and §9 already flag "confirm shipped minimum ages with Stefano" as an
open item, sourced from a generic mismatch between the item master and the producer's site. The
booklet sharpens that into something more specific and more urgent, because it's the regulator's
own naming, not just a number:

| Sign record (`signs.json`) | Ships at | Consortium's own name for that band |
|---|---|---|
| Asiago Fresco | 30–40 days | **Fresco** (20–40 days) — matches cleanly |
| Asiago Fresco di Montagna | 60–70 days | No named band covers this — falls between Fresco's 40-day ceiling and Mezzano's 4-month floor. Not a contradiction (the booklet is a consumer pamphlet, not the full disciplinare), but worth a one-line check with Stefano: does Monti Trentini's own paperwork call this "Fresco," or something else? |
| Asiago Stagionato | 5 months | **Mezzano** (4–6 months) — the sign is accurately aged for what the consortium calls Mezzano, but the sign's own name just says "Stagionato" (the umbrella term). Not wrong, but "Mezzano" is more specific and reads as more expert/authentic if we want it. |
| **Asiago Vecchio** | **9 months** | **Vecchio requires *over 10 months*** per the consortium's own literature. |

**The Vecchio line is the finding to act on.** The sign — and the item master behind it — calls
a 9-month cheese "Vecchio." The body that owns the word "Vecchio" for Asiago DOP defines it as
*over 10 months*. That's not a rounding difference; it's short of the regulator's own minimum by
a month. Three ways this resolves, and only Stefano knows which:

1. **The item master understates the actual age** — Monti Trentini ages it past 10 months and
   "9 months" in our data is stale or approximate. Fix: update `minAge` to the real figure.
2. **It genuinely ships at 9 months and is sold commercially as "Vecchio" anyway** — common enough
   in trade, where "Vecchio" sometimes gets used more loosely than the consortium's own consumer
   messaging suggests. Fix: nothing on the sign, but worth knowing this is a soft spot if a
   customer or inspector ever presses on it.
3. **It should not be called "Vecchio" at all** — if it's reliably 9 months and no more, the
   accurate consortium name for that age is closer to a long Mezzano than a Vecchio. Fix: rename
   the sign and update `signs.json`'s `id`/`name` fields.

Also worth fixing regardless of which of the three is true: `signs.json`'s `dopMinimum` field for
this record currently reads `"9 months (Vecchio)"` — that's circular, it's citing the product's
own claimed age as its own justification rather than an actual regulatory minimum. Once Stefano
answers, that field should hold the real consortium minimum (10 months) for reference, the way
every other record's `dopMinimum` already does correctly (e.g. Fresco's `dopMinimum: "20 days"`
against a shipped `minAge` of "30–40 days").

**Action:** fold this into the existing "shipped minimum ages" question already queued for
Stefano in `CHEESE_SIGNS_SPEC.md` §9 item 2 — it's the same open item, now with a specific,
checkable claim attached instead of a generic "these two sources disagree."

## 3. Using the photos

The 16 reference images are useful for exactly what's transcribed above — reading the regulator's
own language and sensory descriptions accurately, and knowing what the official marks *look like*
(the EU PDO seal's blue/gold sunburst, the casein stamp, the Consorzio's own circular emblem on
the cover). **They are not production art.** They're phone photos of a printed, glossy booklet —
warped pages, glare, halftone dot pattern from the original print job. `CHEESE_SIGNS_SPEC.md` §9
item 3 ("official EU PDO + Asiago consortium mark artwork to replace the house badges") still
needs the real vector files, which should come from the consortium's own press/media kit
(consorzioasiago.it) or a formal request through Marketing per `CLIENT_DATA_ROLES.md` — not a crop
of these photos.

What the photos *are* good for right now: the sensory-recognition language above (sight / smell /
taste / feel / chewing) is regulator-sourced, plain-English, and already broken out by product —
a strong starting point for Media Hub captions, web copy, or an in-store "how to taste it" card,
with far more authority than anything written from scratch.
